# Example Article: High Self-Awareness/Satirical

**Title:** "I Spent 6 Months Building an AI That Predicts Which AI Articles Are Full of Hot Air (Spoiler: It's Most of Them)"

**Author:** Alex Rodriguez, Professional AI Skeptic

---

After reading approximately 847 LinkedIn posts about how GPT-5 will definitely achieve AGI by Tuesday, I decided to build something useful: an AI that detects AI BS. Meta? Absolutely. Necessary? Unfortunately, yes.

The problem is simple: for every thoughtful piece about the real challenges in machine learning, there are seventeen articles claiming someone has "disrupted the entire paradigm" by fine-tuning BERT on their company's Slack messages. The signal-to-noise ratio in AI discourse has become roughly equivalent to a teenager's bedroom.

So I built the Artificial Intelligence Artificial Intelligence Detector (AIAIAD, because acronyms make everything sound more important). It analyzes AI articles for what I call "expertise inflation" - the tendency to sound profound while saying very little.

The technical approach is embarrassingly simple. I trained a classifier on a dataset I painstakingly curated, consisting of:
- 200 actual research papers (labeled "probably legit")
- 500 thought leadership posts (labeled "citation needed")  
- 300 LinkedIn announcements about revolutionary breakthroughs (labeled "please stop")

My sophisticated machine learning pipeline involves counting buzzwords, measuring confidence-to-evidence ratios, and detecting claims that could only be true if the author had secretly achieved time travel.

Early results are promising, if depressing. The model correctly identified 94% of articles that use "paradigm-shifting" unironically. It also flagged every single post that mentions "democratizing AI" without explaining what that actually means.

My favorite discovery: there's a strong correlation between using the phrase "cracked the code" and having absolutely no code to show for it. The model learned this pattern entirely on its own, which either makes it very clever or suggests the pattern is painfully obvious.

I should note that this project started as a joke and somehow became a legitimate research direction. This probably says something profound about the current state of AI discourse, though I'm not sure what.

The irony isn't lost on me that I'm now writing an article about AI while building AI to detect bad AI articles. It's like that movie Inception, but with more transformers and less coherent plotlines.

Will this solve the expertise inflation problem? Probably not. Will it at least help me filter my LinkedIn feed? One can dream.

The code is on GitHub, because apparently that's how we validate everything now. PRs welcome, especially if you can help me figure out why the model thinks this very article scores a 7.2 on my own BS detector.
